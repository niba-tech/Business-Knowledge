The one-paragraph takeaway

Change can't be ordered. It has to be pulled.

If you're trying to get people to change, you've already lost — change ordered from the top triggers the one thing every organization is built to do: protect itself. The immune system treats your new process as an infection and kills it politely, through delay and "let's circle back." Pushing harder only confirms the threat. The way out is pull: rebuild one painfully broken workflow to be 10x better, put it in the hands of your most frustrated person, and let results create envy — because belief follows experience, never the other way around.

The mental model

Push vs. pull — why mandates backfire

The recurring frame in Ismail's argument: a top-down "push" triggers the immune system, while a "pull" made irresistible spreads on its own.

Push — what fails

  • Sell the future with slides and memos — "nobody ever changed their mind because of a slide"
  • Roll out to everyone at once, mandate it
  • Ban the old workflow outright
  • Outcome: polite "yes" in the room, then the immune system attacks through delay and scheduling

Pull — what works

  • Rebuild one workflow AI-native so it's 10x better
  • Put it in front of one drowning person, then get out of the way
  • Remove friction from the new path; let the old path age quietly
  • Outcome: one public win creates envy — the first defector, then the second
Key insights

The immune system, and the power of pull

01

The immune system — change looks like an infection

Every company has an immune system, built to withstand risk and resist change. To it, a new process, tool, or workflow looks like an infection — so it attacks quietly: through delay, "let's circle back," and meetings that schedule change into the next quarter.

To the immune system, your transformation looks like an infection. So it does what the immune system does — it attacks, quietly, politely, through delay.— Salim Ismail
02

Pushing harder confirms the threat

The harder you push, the more you hand the immune system exactly what it needs to mobilize. Change programs fail not because people are lazy or afraid, but because you're fighting a system that is doing its job.

When you push harder, you confirm the threat. You hand the immune system exactly what it needs to mobilize.— Salim Ismail
03

The power of pull

The entire game, as John Hagel and John Seely Brown frame it: don't push people out of the old way — make the new way so good that staying put feels absurd. People abandon the old workflow on their own.

You don't push people out of the old way, you make the new way so good that staying put feels absurd and people pull that to themselves.— Salim Ismail
04

10x, not 10%

Don't rebuild a workflow to be a bit faster or 10% better. People will tolerate a broken process they know; they only abandon it for something that makes their day much easier. Redesign it AI-native so it's 10 times better.

People will tolerate a broken process they know. What they will not do is abandon it for something marginally less broken.— Salim Ismail
05

Envy is the pull mechanism

You don't roll the new way out. You give it to one person — the most frustrated, the one already drowning — and get out of their path. When their Monday gets easier while everyone else's stays hard, you've created envy, and envy is pull.

You've not made an argument, you've created envy. That envy is pull.— Salim Ismail
06

Belief follows experience

You don't change the mindset and then change the work. Change one piece of the work, let people feel it, and the mindset follows — which is why the first win must be real and public.

Belief comes after experience, never before.— Salim Ismail
Concrete application · today

Do it today — the three moves

Ismail's playbook for pulling a team into a new way of working. Start with one workflow; don't try to change everything at once.

1

Pick the workflow quietly costing you the most

One workflow — not the easiest one, the one bleeding money or time. Rebuild it AI-native so it's 10x better, not 10% better; a marginally improved version won't be worth the switch.

Today · 1 hour to choose and scope
2

Hand it to the most frustrated person

Give the new way to the person who's already drowning — not the champion, not the skeptic. Get out of their path and let the results speak for themselves.

This week · set them up, then step back
3

Make the new path easy, let the old path age

Your job changes from selling the future to removing friction from the new path. Don't ban the old workflow — stop defending it, and let it get less convenient on its own.

Ongoing · weekly friction review
4

Watch for the first defector

Don't wait for unanimity — you'll never get it. Watch for the first and second person to visibly switch, and let their public win do the convincing. Movements start with one person, not everybody.

Weekly · celebrate early defections publicly
