The one-paragraph takeaway

Stuck is not a character flaw. It is a visible, untie-able knot.

Almost everyone is stuck — not because they're lazy or broken, but because they are trying to want two things that cannot coexist at the same time (the donkey frozen between water and carrot). Most of what we call "being stuck" is a knot we can't see — the Alexdander-and-Gordian knot insight: you're trapped by a hidden "pole," not by true hopelessness. The way out is to name the conflicting want ("I want X but I also want Y"), forgive the sunk costs from your former self, declare a purpose ("who is this for, and what change am I here to make?"), and serve the smallest viable audience with a practice you control — trusting that quitting the wrong thing is the superpower that frees you to start the right one.

The framework

Use The Knot to diagnose any situation you're in.

Godin's model for "getting unstuck" — a four-part pattern you can run in any life area: a job, a relationship, a hobby, a career.

1  Problem vs. Situation

  • Problems have a solution — find it and act.
  • Situations (gravity, a bad market) cannot be changed — accept them and work within them.
  • Most pain comes from treating a situation like it's a fixable problem, or a problem like it's an unchangeable situation.
  • You can't change Uber — but the rules come with the choice you made. Choose consciously.

2  Find the hidden "But"

  • "I want the creative life, but I don't want to be rejected."
  • "I want to run the marathon, but I don't want to get tired."
  • The "but" usually hides as an "and" — surface it out loud.
  • Once named, you can finally choose which want to serve — that's the "remove the pole" moment.

3  Forgive your former self

  • Your law degree, bad lease, or 4-year sunk cost is a gift you don't have to accept.
  • "Knowing what you know now, would you make this choice again?" If no, stop defending it.
  • Pay the exit price if needed — it's cheaper than punishing yourself with the next two years of your life.

4  Declare a purpose & serve who it's for

  • You aren't born with a purpose — you choose one, with intent, for the next cycle.
  • "Who is this for? What change are you here to make?"
  • Work backward from the smallest viable audience — 5, 12, or 25 people is enough.
  • Keep the promise you made to them, over and over. That's the practice.

🟢 The Dip → keep going

  • The hard part between starting and being good (organic chem → doctor).
  • Ask: has anyone ever made it through this dip before? If yes, it's a dip.
  • See it before you start; when it arrives, say "thank you" — you've reached the signpost.

🔴 The Cul-de-sac → quit

  • A dead end where more effort only makes you worse (smoking with emphysema).
  • Ask: is there evidence more money/time reaches the other side? If no evidence — quit.
  • Quitting the right thing is a superpower, not a flaw.
Key insights

Twelve ideas that reframe how you think about quitting, permission, and work.

01

"Never quit" is wrong. Strategic quitting is a superpower.

Michael Jordan's gym-wall slogan is demonstrably false — he doesn't play pro basketball, baseball, or golf anymore. He quit when the thing stopped being productive. Quitting the wrong thing is what allows you to start the right one.

"If you quit once, it becomes a habit. Never quit." He's wrong, and it's very clear that he's wrong.— Seth Godin (about the MJ quote)
02

You're the donkey between the water and the carrot.

Buridan's Donkey: completely free, yet starving to death because he can't move toward one want without moving away from the other. Every "I want this but I also want that" freeze you feel is this donkey — and the escape is to name the "but" out loud.

It's when we want two things at the same time that cannot coexist that we feel stuck.— The exact mechanics of stuckness
03

Find the "pole" of your knot.

Alexander the Great didn't cut the Gordian knot — he removed the pole it was wrapped around and the whole thing fell apart. Your "pole" is the hidden structural cause: a measured-too-late outcome, a status chase, an invisible rule. Remove it and the knot unties.

We are stuck because we can't see what's holding us there.— The whole book, in one line
04

Sunk costs are gifts from your former self.

The law degree, the expensive lease, the four years in the wrong career — they're a gift someone you used to be gave you. You don't have to accept gifts. "Knowing what I now know, would I choose this again?" If no, thank that former self and untie the knot.

Most of us regret the things we didn't do far more than the things we tried and failed at.— Regret as fuel, not shame
05

Stop waiting to be picked. Pick yourself.

The old system (do well in school, a company picks you, get promoted, die) was rigged — and the gates are gone. A camera, a podcast mic, an AI: the tools of production cost nothing now. Steven started this podcast from his kitchen after LBC never called back.

We live in a permission-seeking state because it's a good shield.— Waiting to be picked is a comfortable excuse for resistance
06

Serve the smallest viable audience.

Start with the 5–25 people who would miss you. Make a difference for one person you know who'd love it, then scale from there. "Michelle Obama won't be on your podcast — ask your next-door neighbor first."

Make promises and keep them for the smallest audience. That's enough.— Why "go big or go home" is a trap
07

Resistance is a nameable, danceable demon.

Writer's block, changing outfits six times before a date, "I've been thinking about my podcast for two years" — all resistance: fear disguised as preparation. You can't make it go away; you name it, invite it in for tea, shrink the ask to the smallest unit (an anonymous blog for 100 days), and go.

Anyone could have done that. No one does. Resistance.— The potluck story: 3 people → 400 friends in 50 weeks
08

Embarrassment is the cost of entry. Shame is opt-in.

Anything new outside your current identity costs embarrassment — pay it early and often. And shame only works if you receive it. The lifeguard metaphor: someone is drowning in front of you; you don't apologize for your stroke — you save the kid.

Shame is this interesting emotion because we must receive it. You can simply take it seriously — not personally.— The 4-year-old on the playground can't shame you through a fence
09

Perfectionism is a form of procrastination. Quality = meets spec.

A 1998 Toyota Celica was higher quality than a 1998 Rolls-Royce — the parts fit together. If the result doesn't meet spec, tighten the spec. "Perfect" is unachievable, which makes perfectionism the perfect excuse to never ship.

If I announce I'm a perfectionist, what I've announced is that I'm never going to need to ship this.— Quality is not luxury — it's consistency
10

Authenticity is for amateurs. Professionals are consistent.

Your friends and family want you "authentic." Everyone else wants you consistent — making a promise and keeping it. You don't want an "authentically bad mood" surgeon mid-knee-surgery. Play the best version of yourself on stage, and let the real you be for the people who actually know you.

Consistency must rhyme with your authentic self. And your authentic self changes based on the promises you keep.— How "authentic" is a Trojan horse for hiding
11

The juggling secret: get good at throwing, not catching.

Everyone lunges for the catch and blames the process. Juggling is actually about throwing — if the throw is right, the catches take care of themselves. Build the practice (the thing under your control) and stop bargaining with outcomes (the thing that isn't).

If we can develop a practice, we're no longer attached to the outcome because the outcome is out of our control. The practice is under our control.— Process over results
12

Generous tension is the engine of forward motion.

To launch anything you must create tension — not relieve it. Taylor Swift creates FOMO for 40,000 people who can't fit in the arena. It's awkward to say "I'm doing this thing," but it's the only way to move others — and yourself — forward.

The only way to launch something new is to create tension. Generous tension.— FOMO itself is tension, and tension is a gift
The AI section

Either you work for AI, or AI works for you.

"This is the biggest change in our world since electricity." The 1890 people who waited for electricity to go away did not work out well. This time it's much, much faster.

Seth on AI (from 1983 Stanford AI coursework to now)

The rule of thumb: if you can write down what you do all day, an AI can learn to do it faster and cheaper. You want a job you can't write down — that's where you're irreplaceable.
The movie theater test: electricity was delivered to everyone, but it took a movie theater to invent a way to get you to pay for it. AI is electricity — the businesses that win find the new "movie theater."
The unpriceable assets: good taste, trust, networks of people, empathy, judgment, taste about who to connect and how. "AI is never going to replace networks of people."
Race to the top: the bosses cutting costs with AI are racing to the bottom. The winners use AI to be more human — doctors who spend more time per patient, not less.
The loneliness paradox: networks made of people create value and belonging; social media networks increase loneliness. Connect human to human, and you'll never struggle to make a living.

If you're feeling AI pressure right now

  • Stop trying to be a "junior OpenAI."
  • Use the tools to serve a specific, small group of people you know and trust.
  • Let AI handle the "catchable" parts; double down on the connections only you can hold.
  • Ask: who needs me — my taste, my history, my network — to do something they can't?
Agency & control

Feeling in control changes outcomes — measurably.

DATA

The control studies

People who believe they control their lives rate satisfaction 7.4/10 vs. 5.6 for those who don't. In the famous 1976 nursing-home study, residents given a plant and daily choices were 50% less likely to die over 18 months. And in hospital, patients with a painkiller button used equal or less medication while reporting lower pain — the stress of helplessness amplifies pain itself.

The physical pain doesn't change. The stress of helplessness amplifies it.— Control is medicine
SHIFT

Helplessness is (at least partly) a story.

Unfair systems are real — Godin grants that fully. But within any situation, ask: what do I actually control? The story we tell ourselves about a day changes which choices we see. "It's not about what you want to accomplish — it's about what you wish you'd done."

Our leverage, our agency, is to be able to say: there's a thing I could do today that my future self will thank me for.— Agency → responsibility → choice
DRIVERS

Three forces drive every decision: fear, affiliation, status.

Fear ("freedom from fear" = a cage), affiliation (fitting in — you don't wear a clown suit to a funeral), and status (who eats first at the oasis). Decode the "but" in your head and you'll usually find one of these three. The car you want isn't about speed — it's about the kid at the lunch table.

Name it. I want this car because that kid at high school could never afford this car.— What problem are you actually solving?
Concrete application · today

The 6-step unhooking ritual — run it this afternoon.

Each step takes 10–15 minutes. Together they're the practical engine of the whole conversation.

1

Pick the one knot, on paper.

Job, relationship, project, identity. Write it down with a pen. Define its boundary — "this thing, not everything."

Do today · 5 min
2

Write out the "but."

"I want X but I also want Y." Force the two incompatible wants to appear together. Say it out loud to someone. It feels ridiculous — that's the point.

Do today · 5 min
3

Separate problem from situation.

Can this be changed? If yes → problem, seek the solution. If no → situation, make peace and work within it. Stop burning energy on the wrong bucket.

Do today · 5 min
4

Forgive the sunk cost.

List what you'd "lose" by quitting. Reframe each as a gift from your former self. "Knowing what I know now, would I choose this again?" Let the no be an answer.

Do today · 10 min
5

Name who it's for — and shrink it.

You're not serving "everyone." Pick 5, 12, or 25 real people. What do they believe, want, and fear? What's the smallest gift you can make for them?

Do today · 15 min
6

Begin the practice — ship the smallest unit.

An anonymous blog for 100 days. Ask the next-door neighbor to be your podcast guest. Throw the first ball. The outcome is not yours; the practice is. Start it tonight.

Do today · start tonight

A faster daily version, once the ritual is familiar: Name the but → separate problem from situation → forgive the sunk cost → serve the smallest audience → ship the smallest unit. Run this loop weekly on your job, your side project, and your relationships. It's how you "become the person your future self thanks you for."

If you're in a career you've outgrown

  • Name the "but": fulfilling-but-insecure vs. stable-but-emptied.
  • You can choose to fully accept the job you're in — the undertaker who decides to be the best undertaker.
  • Or lower the cost of exit: change the house, the car, the status markers that lock you in.
  • Remember: "hire a boss" — don't wait to be picked by the next one.

If it's a relationship that's over

  • "That relationship was then — I made the right calls with who I was then."
  • Question is forward-only: will recommitting pay off in the future?
  • If no, thank your former self for the gift and close it with grace.
  • Don't let the fear of "wasted years" double your waste.
